Need a breath first?
  1. Plant your feet. Drop your shoulders. Slow, long exhale.
  2. Swap out if you can: “Tag, I need a minute.”
  3. Say one thing, then be quiet: “You are very upset. I am here. I will not let you hit.”

    The plan, in short

    During a tantrum
    • Move your older child to his safe spot. Quietly clear throwable things. Stay out of kicking range.
    • Few words: “Angry is okay. Hitting is not.” “I'll stay close. We'll talk when your body is safe.”
    • If breathing is refused: “You don't have to. I'll stay nearby.” Offer one choice once, then stop.
    • Keep a reasonable “no”. Add no new punishment. No lectures or bargaining.
    When he's calm
    • “That was a big upset. I love you.” Small repair: wipe up together.
    • Practise “Help me” or “I need space” once. Praise the first safe behaviour.
    • Then back to routine. No long lecture.
    For us, and his brother
    • If we lose it: “That was not a safe choice, and I am sorry. Next time I will step back and get help.”
    • To the older child: “That was loud. You did nothing wrong. It's not your job to fix his feelings.”
    • Protect his bedtime. Give him one-to-one time every day.
    When to get help
    • Danger to anyone: call 112 / 999.
    • Book the GP or public health nurse. Bring this log. Ask about Parents Plus.
    • Mention promptly: self-injury, injuries to others, episodes at preschool, lost skills.
